From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, the sequence is strict: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower sending heat through the house. Diagnosis is mostly a matter of finding where that chain breaks. In Brentwood homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Brentwood HVAC repairs furnaces by testing the sequence, not guessing at it. Our experienced technicians watch the startup step by step and measure at each stage: igniter resistance, flame signal in microamps, pressure switch operation against actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor health. Every visit includes the safety fundamentals on a fuel-burning appliance: the heat exchanger inspected, connections leak-tested, and draft confirmed, because the difference between a furnace problem and a furnace hazard is not visible from the thermostat. We measure temperature rise against the data plate before we leave so the fire and the airflow are matched the way the manufacturer intended.

We work on gas, propane, and electric models from every major brand, both conventional 80 percent units and condensing high-efficiency models with their traps, drains, and dual pressure switches. The truck stock covers the parts that actually fail: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors, so most no-heat calls end with heat the same visit. When to Call for Furnace Repair in Brentwood

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Keep an eye out for the following clues that it's time to bring in an expert:

  • Water, rust stains, or black soot around the flue or cabinet
  • Yellow, wavering burner flames where they should be sharp blue
  • A furnace that shuts off every few minutes before the house warms up
  • The blower runs constantly but the air never warms
  • Clicking and a glowing igniter with no burner flame
  • Flames that start, last a few seconds, and go out again and again
  • Squealing or grinding from the blower compartment
  • A bang or boom when the burners ignite late

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

A furnace combines gas, flame, high voltage, and a flue in one cabinet, and the failure that matters most is the one you cannot see from outside: a compromised heat exchanger passing combustion gases into the air your blower pushes through the house. Delayed ignition that booms at light-off stresses that exchanger further every morning it goes unfixed. Meanwhile the diagnostic traps stack up: a furnace that trips its high limit may have a bad switch, or it may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a dying blower, and replacing the switch in the second case just removes a safety while leaving the fault.

Our experienced technicians treat every symptom as a question about the whole system. Flame signal gets measured rather than assumed, so a healthy sensor is never replaced while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve skates by. Draft is verified at the pressure switch and the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, which is how airflow problems get caught before they shorten the exchanger's life. And the gas side is leak-tested with instruments, not a sniff and a shrug. The result is a furnace that has been proven safe and correct under a full cycle, and an honest report if the unit is nearing the point where replacement beats another repair.

How to Prevent Furnace Repair Problems

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Avoid packing the utility room, because burners need open space for combustion air
  • Replace the filter every month while the furnace is working hard, since it costs less than any other part
  • Test your CO detectors monthly and make sure each floor has one
  • Keep the condensate trap and drain clear on high-efficiency units to prevent lockouts
  • Notice how the furnace sounds at startup, because a quiet light-off is healthy and a boom is not
  • Schedule service each fall to catch worn igniters and dirty sensors before cold weather arrives
  • Store laundry supplies, especially chlorine products, well away from the furnace closet
